from motionmaxxing's `references/idea.md` (Apache-2.0, github.com/Tejashmakwana/motionmaxxing). This file is under the Apache License 2.0 (LICENSES/Apache-2.0.txt). See NOTICE.

## 1. Order of decisions

Story, then frames, then style, then copy, then motion. Choosing an effect before the story is
locked is the commonest way a film goes wrong. 15 s holds 3-5 beats and one idea; 30 s holds
6-10 beats and still one idea. Leaving things out is the point.

Find the one true thing: if the brief names a feature, look at the real thing (docs, changelog,
the UI). If it names only a brand, pick one real feature, one real behaviour, one real moment.
A real example, number or time of day beats a general claim.

## 2. Where the idea hides (the subject inventory)

One line each in your notes; "none" is an answer.

| Look at | Ask | What tends to come out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The logo's shape | What object or gesture does it resemble? (a pill is a toggle, a ring is progress) | the mark becomes a working part of the film, not a sticker at the end |
| The core control | What does a user touch? (play, search, slider, send, toggle) | the film's one cause and effect lives on it |
| The signature material | What fills their screen or shelf? (maps, code, receipts, photos, paper) | that material is the ground and the hero image |
| The key verb | sort, ship, split, find, track | motion performs the verb, on screen and on the key word |
| The success moment | what is on screen when it works? | build the film backwards from that frame |
| The voice | read their headlines aloud: dry, warm, cocky, exact? | copy and timing sound like that |
| The colour logic | which colour means on, yours, new, error? | colours keep those meanings in the film |
| What they would never do | a calm brand does not glitch | the edges you respect |

Keep literal: the logo (never redraw it), typeface, colour values, real UI, real copy, real
behaviour. Invent: the stage, camera, light, metaphor and choreography, drawn in the subject's
own grammar (its corner radius, stroke weight, pace).

## 3. Kinds of film (the three directions must be different kinds)

| Kind | Shape | Works when | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Documentary of one real task | one specific case, question to result; the camera explains | it is a tool people do something in | it turns into a feature tour |
| One held shot that slowly changes | one subject, something changes under the camera | one object or material carries the brand | the world is empty |
| A joke with a character | a small figure, a deadpan problem, the product as straight man | the problem is funny and relatable | the character comes from the category's clip-art |
| Typographic essay | words are the picture; each performs its meaning | the value is a sentence and the voice is strong | it becomes a centred title page |
| Montage of real output | many real results cut to a rhythm | the product makes things | the output is invented or samey |
| Stillness that breaks | calm, one visible cause breaks it, calm again | the brand sells relief or focus | the break has no cause |
| Claim, cut, proof | a claim in type, a hard cut to the product doing it | a one-line promise with a demo | it is used because it is the default |
| Transformation chain | A becomes B becomes C becomes the logo | only when the chain is the product | it is picked because it is the film you always make |

## 4. The two tests (run on every direction)

- **Cover the logo.** Does the film still say whose it is?
- **Swap in a competitor.** Would it work for them with the colours changed? If yes, it is the
  category's film, not an idea yet.

The direction also has to predict its hero moment. If you cannot name the frame, you have a
topic, not an idea.

## 5. Abstract words into pictures

Ask what the word looks like, or what it is the opposite of. Use each metaphor once per film.
"Top 1%" can be 100 identical dots where all but one dim. "Over a weekend" can be a calendar whose
last two days light up. Take props from the audience's world, not the category's clip-art: no
rockets for launch, shields for security or light bulbs for ideas.

With a script or VO, mark each line's cue before designing: numbers get a visual built from the
number; abstract nouns get a concrete metaphor; names get their own hold; contrast words ("but",
"instead") get a turn (a change of ground, scale or camera). Mute test: does the picture alone
tell the same story? Audio-only test: is anything missing that the picture should carry?

## 6. Copy

Write like the subject speaks, then cut half. Their real phrasing beats a stock line. Real UI text
beats marketing text. Avoid lines that could be pasted into any launch: "Not just X. It's Y.",
one-word triplets ("Faster. Smarter. Yours."), "Unlock", "Meet X", "the future of", "seamless",
and "Get started" as the last line. Do not invent stats, quotes or users.
